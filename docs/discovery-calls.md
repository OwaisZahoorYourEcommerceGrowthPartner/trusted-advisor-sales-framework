# Discovery Calls

Discovery is not a script. It is the process of understanding the business well enough to earn the right to recommend a solution.

## Discovery Objective

The goal is to understand:

1. What the buyer wants
2. Why it matters now
3. What has already been tried
4. What is blocking progress
5. What decision would make sense next

## The Best Discovery Questions

### Business Context

- What is the main priority for this channel right now?
- Are you optimizing for revenue, profit, market share, or operational stability?
- What would make this channel feel successful over the next 90 days?
- What changed recently that made this more important?

### Current Setup

- Who is currently managing this?
- What is working well today?
- What is not working as well as expected?
- Where do you feel the biggest uncertainty is?

### Economics

- What does profitable growth look like for this channel?
- Is there a target margin or break-even threshold we should respect?
- Are promotions, fees, shipping, or inventory affecting the advertising strategy?
- Are there products you want to protect, scale, or avoid pushing?

### Decision Process

- Who else is involved in deciding whether to make changes?
- What would make you confident enough to move forward?
- Is the blocker budget, timing, trust, strategy, or operational bandwidth?

## What To Listen For

The words matter less than the pattern.

Listen for:

- Urgency
- Frustration
- Uncertainty
- Internal disagreement
- Existing vendor concerns
- Strategic transitions
- Profitability pressure
- Growth targets

## Discovery Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes:

- Pitching before understanding the problem
- Attacking the current manager
- Talking only about your skills
- Asking questions that sound generic
- Treating every objection as a price objection
- Trying to force a decision before timing is right

## Strong Discovery Close

A good discovery close sounds like this:

> Based on what you shared, I would not recommend changing everything immediately. I would first want to review the data and identify whether this is a structure issue, a profitability issue, or a scaling issue. If the findings are useful, then we can decide whether it makes sense to work together.

This keeps pressure low and trust high.
