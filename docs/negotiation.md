# Negotiation

Negotiation starts before price is discussed.

If the buyer sees you as labor, they negotiate hours.

If the buyer sees you as judgment, they negotiate outcomes.

## Anchor Around Value

Do not rush to quote the lowest number.

Before pricing, understand:

- Account size
- Complexity
- Current pain
- Growth potential
- Level of responsibility
- Strategic importance
- Expected communication cadence

## Price With Scope

A price without scope is easy to reject.

A better structure is:

> For this type of work, I usually structure it as a monthly retainer depending on spend, complexity, and the level of strategic involvement needed.

This gives you room to price properly.

## Avoid Desperate Discounts

Do not discount just because the buyer pauses.

Silence is not always price resistance.

It may be:

- Internal discussion
- Busy schedule
- Strategic uncertainty
- Current vendor politics
- Low urgency

Discounting too early trains the buyer to wait.

## Better Than Discounting

If the buyer pushes back, adjust scope before price.

Example:

> If that does not fit the current budget, we can reduce the scope to a monthly strategy review and priority roadmap rather than full management.

This protects your positioning.

## Performance Trials

A trial can work if it is framed correctly.

Weak framing:

> I will work for free.

Strong framing:

> I can start with a short risk-reversal period focused on diagnosing and improving the highest-priority areas. If the work does not create meaningful value, there is no obligation to continue.

The second version signals confidence.

## Pricing Language

Use:

- Typical engagements
- Based on scope
- Depending on complexity
- If the opportunity is mainly strategic
- We can align the structure after reviewing the account

Avoid:

- Whatever you can pay
- I am flexible
- I can do cheap
- Just give me a chance
- I need the work

## The Best Negotiation Position

The best negotiation position is not pretending you do not care.

It is genuinely creating value before the buyer makes a decision.

When the buyer has already seen your thinking, pricing becomes easier.
