# Audit-Led Selling Framework

An audit is not a list of problems.

An audit is a trust-building asset.

It should make the buyer think:

> This person sees the business more clearly than most people we talk to.

## What A Strong Audit Does

A strong audit:

- Diagnoses the business problem
- Connects tactical issues to financial outcomes
- Prioritizes what matters most
- Avoids unnecessary blame
- Creates a clear next step

## What A Weak Audit Does

A weak audit says:

- This campaign has poor ROAS
- Add negatives
- Increase bids
- Improve listings
- Test new keywords

Those may be true, but they are generic.

A strong audit explains what the data means for the business.

## The Audit Narrative

Every audit should have a clear narrative.

Examples:

- The account has a traffic problem.
- The account has a conversion problem.
- The account has a profitability problem.
- The account has a capital allocation problem.
- The account has a structure problem.
- The account has a product mix problem.

Do not make the buyer assemble the story themselves.

## The Five Questions

A strong audit answers:

1. Where is money being lost?
2. Where is growth being limited?
3. What is under-leveraged?
4. What should be fixed first?
5. Why does this matter financially?

## Recommended Sections

### 1. Executive Summary

One page. No clutter.

Include:

- Main diagnosis
- Top 3 findings
- Top 3 opportunities
- Immediate recommendation

### 2. Business Context

Explain what the account appears to be optimized for.

Examples:

- Revenue over profit
- Traffic over conversion
- Discovery over efficiency
- Short-term volume over durable growth

### 3. Capital Allocation Review

Show where money is going.

Ask:

- Are top campaigns receiving too much spend?
- Are winners underfunded?
- Are poor performers protected by catch-all structures?
- Are hero products getting the support they deserve?

### 4. Search Intent Analysis

Segment traffic into:

- Brand
- Category
- Competitor
- Noise
- Product-specific

This is more powerful than looking at keywords one by one.

### 5. Product Portfolio Review

Classify products into:

- Heroes: high sales, strong economics
- Sleepers: low spend, strong ROAS
- Zombies: spending but not producing
- Defenders: important products that need protection

### 6. Action Roadmap

Break the plan into:

- 72-hour actions
- 30-day rebuild
- 90-day scale plan

## Audit Language That Converts

Instead of:

> This campaign is bad.

Say:

> This campaign may be absorbing budget that could be redeployed into higher-efficiency traffic.

Instead of:

> Your current manager is wasting money.

Say:

> The current structure makes it difficult to separate profitable intent from discovery traffic.

Instead of:

> I can fix this.

Say:

> If I were responsible for this P&L, I would first recover efficiency, then scale from a stronger economic base.

## The Executive Decision Page

End every serious audit with an executive decision page.

| Decision | Why It Matters | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Reallocate spend from low-efficiency traffic | Improves profitability before increasing budget | Critical |
| Isolate proven search intent | Makes winners easier to scale | High |
| Review product-level economics | Prevents scaling low-margin volume | High |
| Create weekly profitability reviews | Maintains discipline | Medium |

This turns the audit into a boardroom tool.

## Final Rule

The audit should not prove that you are smart.

It should prove that you can help the buyer make better decisions.
