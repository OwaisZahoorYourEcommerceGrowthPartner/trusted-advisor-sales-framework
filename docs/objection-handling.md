# Objection Handling

Objection handling is not about winning an argument.

It is about understanding what kind of risk the buyer is trying to avoid.

## The Four Types Of Objections

### 1. Timing Objection

Example:

> We are not making changes right now.

Meaning:

The buyer may be interested, but another priority is more urgent.

Best response:

> That makes sense. I appreciate the context. If the timing changes or you want another perspective later, I would be happy to help.

### 2. Current Vendor Objection

Example:

> We already have someone managing this.

Meaning:

The buyer does not want disruption unless the current setup creates enough pain.

Best response:

> Completely understand. I would not want to disrupt something that is working. If helpful, I can provide a second perspective and identify whether there are any areas worth reviewing.

### 3. Trust Objection

Example:

> How would you actually grow this?

Meaning:

The buyer is not asking for credentials. They are asking for your thinking.

Best response:

> Before making promises, I would want to review the current data. My process would focus on profitability, budget allocation, search intent quality, and the highest-leverage opportunities. The goal would not be to simply manage activity, but to improve the economics of the channel.

### 4. Price Objection

Example:

> That is more than we expected.

Meaning:

The buyer is unsure whether the value justifies the price.

Best response:

> I understand. I do not want this to be viewed as just a management fee. The value would come from improving decision-making, reducing waste, and creating a clearer path to profitable growth. If the scope needs to be adjusted, we can discuss that.

## The Rule

Never respond to an objection with insecurity.

Do not over-explain.

Do not discount immediately.

Do not become defensive.

Pause. Identify the real objection. Respond to that.

## Objection Response Formula

1. Acknowledge the concern.
2. Validate the buyer's perspective.
3. Reframe around business value.
4. Offer a low-pressure next step.

Example:

> That makes complete sense. I would not recommend changing the current setup just for the sake of making a change. The only reason to review this would be if there is a clear opportunity to improve profitability or growth. If you ever want a second perspective, I would be happy to take a look.

## What Not To Do

Do not say:

- I can do better than them.
- You should replace your current manager.
- I need this opportunity.
- I will do it cheaper.
- Just give me a chance.

Those phrases reduce trust.

A trusted advisor does not beg for a shot.

A trusted advisor earns the right to be considered.
