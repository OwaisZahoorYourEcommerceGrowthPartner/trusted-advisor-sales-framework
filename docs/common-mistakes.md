# Common Mistakes

## 1. Trying To Close Too Early

If the buyer has not yet trusted your thinking, pushing for a close creates resistance.

Earn access first.

## 2. Treating Every Objection As Price

When a buyer pauses, it does not automatically mean your price is too high.

It may mean timing, trust, internal strategy, or vendor disruption.

## 3. Attacking The Current Manager

Even if the current setup is weak, do not make the buyer feel foolish for choosing it.

Attack the problem, not the person.

## 4. Sending Generic Audits

Generic audits are forgettable.

If the audit could apply to any account, it is not good enough.

## 5. Overclaiming

Do not pretend to know net profit if you do not have margin, fees, shipping, and COGS.

Use language like:

> The current advertising economics suggest profitability pressure.

That is more credible than guessing.

## 6. Using Too Much Tactical Language

A founder may not care about every bid or keyword.

They care about what those details mean for the business.

Translate the data.

## 7. Following Up Out Of Anxiety

The buyer can feel when a follow-up exists only because you are nervous.

Follow up when you have something useful to add.

## 8. Discounting Yourself Too Quickly

If you lower price before the buyer objects, you negotiate against yourself.

## 9. Making The Buyer Choose Too Soon

Sometimes the best move is to let the buyer process.

Trust requires space.

## 10. Forgetting The Long Game

A deal that does not close today can still become a future opportunity.

Your job is to leave the relationship stronger.
